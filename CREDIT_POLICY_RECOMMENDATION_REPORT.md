# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

LendItEazy's portfolio shows elevated credit stress, with **2,460** defaults and an overall default rate of **20.5%**. Risk is concentrated in specific borrower segments, product lines, acquisition channels, and geographies rather than being evenly spread across the loan book. This creates a clear opportunity for targeted credit policy tightening instead of broad-based lending contraction.

The most material risk concentration is in Gig Workers, who default at **32.9%**, almost **3x** the Salaried borrower default rate of **11.0%**. BNPL is the highest-risk product with a **27.7%** default rate and an average ticket size of **₹31,460**, creating a collections economics issue on small-ticket exposure. Social Media is the weakest acquisition channel, defaulting at **30.8%** versus **9.1%** for Referral customers, a **3.4x** risk differential. Tier 3 borrowers default at **25.6%** versus **14.7%** for Tier 1 borrowers, with Gig Worker + Tier 3 reaching the worst observed default rate of **35.2%**.

Early-warning analysis indicates that deterioration is visible before default. DPD divergence appears from month **3**, becomes material by month **6**, and stress signals appear an estimated **60 days** before default for a large share of eventual defaulters. A structured early-warning framework can therefore shift LendItEazy from reactive collections to proactive intervention.

I recommend that LendItEazy implement a **Risk-Adjusted Lending Policy Reset** covering four actions: tighter Gig Worker underwriting, revised BNPL eligibility, reduced dependence on Social Media acquisition, and geography-based pricing for Tier 3 borrowers. If implemented within the proposed timeline, the policy reset is expected to reduce overall default rate from **20.5%** to **13 to 15%** within **12 months**.

# SECTION 1: RISK SEGMENTATION FINDINGS

## 1.1 Employment Type Is the Primary Risk Driver

Analysis reveals that employment type is the strongest borrower-level driver of default risk. Gig Workers default at **32.9%**, materially higher than Students at **22.7%**, Business Owners at **20.9%**, Self Employed borrowers at **12.5%**, and Salaried borrowers at **11.0%**. The gap between Gig Workers and Salaried borrowers is approximately **3x**, confirming that current underwriting does not adequately reflect income stability.

The affordability profile explains the severity of the risk. Gig Workers carry an average EMI-to-income ratio of **68%**, compared with **19.3%** for Salaried borrowers. A borrower spending more than two-thirds of monthly income on EMI has limited ability to absorb income volatility, medical expense, rent pressure, or platform-work seasonality. This indicates structural unaffordability at origination, not only weak repayment behaviour after disbursement.

## 1.2 BNPL Is a High-Volume, High-Loss Product

BNPL has the highest product-level default rate at **27.7%**, compared with Education Loan at **22.8%**, SME Loan at **18.9%**, and Personal Loan at **17.9%**. The product has an average ticket size of **₹31,460**, which creates an unfavourable collections equation: when a small-ticket loan defaults, recovery cost can consume a large share of recoverable value.

The current BNPL policy appears to allow exposure growth before repayment behaviour is sufficiently proven. This is a material risk for a digital lending NBFC because BNPL can scale quickly through app-based approvals and repeat usage. Without stricter eligibility and limit progression rules, BNPL will continue to dilute portfolio quality.

## 1.3 Acquisition Channel Is a Leading Risk Indicator

The acquisition channel materially separates borrower quality. Social Media sourced loans default at **30.8%**, compared with **22.5%** for DSA Agent, **15.1%** for Organic, and **9.1%** for Referral. The Social Media versus Referral gap is **3.4x**, making channel source a practical leading indicator for approval policy.

Social Media also generated **1,286** defaults in the analysis period. This is not only a high default percentage; it is a major absolute loss contributor. Current growth from Social Media should therefore be evaluated on risk-adjusted contribution, not disbursal volume or acquisition cost alone.

## 1.4 Geography Creates Pricing Misalignment

City tier analysis shows clear geographic risk dispersion. Tier 3 borrowers default at **25.6%**, compared with **20.5%** for Tier 2 and **14.7%** for Tier 1. If these borrowers are priced within similar interest bands, Tier 1 profitability effectively cross-subsidises Tier 3 losses.

The risk becomes more severe when geography is combined with employment type. Gig Worker + Tier 3 borrowers default at **35.2%**, the worst observed combination in the portfolio. By contrast, Salaried + Tier 1 borrowers default at **10.6%**, showing that the current portfolio contains both attractive and unattractive risk cells. Policy should therefore become more granular.

# SECTION 2: EARLY WARNING SIGNAL LOGIC

## 2.1 Identified Early-Warning Signals

LendItEazy's data shows that borrower deterioration can be detected before default. The recommended early-warning framework should combine repayment behaviour, cash-flow decline, affordability stress, account volatility, and debt stacking.

| Signal | Threshold | Predictive Strength |
|---|---:|---|
| DPD in months 1-3 | **Above 15 days** | Strong |
| Monthly inflow drop | **Falls 30%+** | Strong |
| EMI-to-income ratio | **Above 60%** | Strong |
| Balance volatility | Shifts to High | Moderate |
| New loan taken | While existing loan is active | Moderate |

DPD trend analysis supports the case for early intervention. Eventual defaulters average **1.4** DPD in month **1**, **5.2** in month **3**, **20.5** in month **6**, and **50.4** in month **12**. Non-defaulters also show some delay, but at lower levels: **0.98**, **3.3**, **12.5**, and **30.7** DPD over the same months. The widening gap from month **3** provides a practical intervention window.

## 2.2 Recommended Intervention Framework

The early-warning framework should operate as a three-tier monitoring system.

| Tier | Trigger | Required Action |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 Watch List | **1 signal present** | Automated reminders and digital nudges; no manual intervention. |
| Tier 2 Active Monitoring | **2 signals present** | Outbound call within **7 days**, restructuring offer, and monthly review. |
| Tier 3 Immediate Intervention | **3 or more signals present** | Priority escalation, immediate restructuring, and suspension of credit limit increases. |

This framework avoids overreaction to one isolated signal while ensuring that compounding borrower stress receives immediate action. Targeted intervention on flagged accounts is expected to reduce defaults by **20 to 25%** within the monitored population, provided that Tier 2 and Tier 3 actions are executed within defined service timelines.

# SECTION 3: POLICY RECOMMENDATION TO THE CRO

## 3.1 Recommended Policy: Risk-Adjusted Lending Policy Reset

Mr. Mehta, I recommend approving a **Risk-Adjusted Lending Policy Reset** as the central policy response to the portfolio stress identified above. The policy should not be treated as a broad reduction in lending appetite. It should be implemented as a targeted risk-control framework focused on the segments where default rates materially exceed portfolio average.

The reset should include four mandatory policy changes.

## 3.2 Tighten Gig Worker Approval Criteria

- Cap unsecured loan exposure at **₹50,000** for Gig Workers.
- Require minimum **6 months** of verifiable income proof.
- Apply a minimum credit score of **650**.
- Price Gig Worker loans at a **4 to 6%** premium over Salaried borrower rates.

Rationale: Gig Workers default at **32.9%** and carry average EMI-to-income of **68%**, making the current approval structure unsustainable without tighter exposure and affordability controls.

## 3.3 Revise BNPL Eligibility Thresholds

- Enforce minimum credit score of **650** for BNPL.
- Cap first BNPL exposure at **₹10,000** for new customers.
- Increase limits only after successful repayment history.
- Restrict BNPL access for customers with **2 or more** active loans.

Rationale: BNPL defaults at **27.7%** with an average ticket of **₹31,460**, requiring smaller initial exposure and repayment-tested limit increases.

## 3.4 Reallocate Acquisition Spend from Social Media

- Reduce Social Media acquisition budget by **40%**.
- Reinvest savings in Referral program incentives.
- Apply a minimum credit score of **620** for Social Media acquired borrowers.
- Track channel-level default rates monthly by vintage.

Rationale: Social Media defaults at **30.8%** versus Referral at **9.1%**, confirming that current channel mix is weakening portfolio quality.

## 3.5 Implement Geography-Based Risk Pricing

- Apply a **4 to 6%** rate premium for Tier 3 borrowers.
- Tighten approval criteria for Gig Worker + Tier 3 applicants.
- Review Tier 3 exposure and pricing adequacy quarterly.

Rationale: Tier 3 borrowers default at **25.6%** versus Tier 1 at **14.7%**, indicating that uniform pricing is creating cross-subsidisation.

## 4.3 Model Limitations and Caveats

- Co-borrower policy showed no meaningful impact, with default rates of **20.4%** with co-borrower and **20.5%** without co-borrower.
